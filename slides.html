The problem

Ponds lose their fertilizer. Fields go thirsty next door.

  • In the Bajo Lempa and the Bay of Jiquilisco, many producers farm a fish or shrimp pond beside a crop field.
  • Pond water is drained and wasted, while the field needs water and nitrogen.
  • Rain is less predictable, and brackish water can salt the soil.

The idea

The pond is a water source with fertilizer included.

PondToPlot answers one daily question — what do I do with my pond water today? — and explains why.

Irrigate with pond waterAmmonia in a useful range and a field that needs water or vigor.
Wait for rainRain is coming or the soil is already wet: save water and pumping.
Exchange or aerateLow oxygen, bad pH or toxic ammonia; fresh drain water goes to the field. Salty water never does.

Rotation & impact

Water from the pond changes what and when you plant

Dry season (Nov–Apr)Fresh pond water = irrigation + nitrogen → nutrient‑hungry crops like maize and vegetables. Brackish pond → low‑water crops or a cover crop.
Rainy season (May–Oct)Rain does the watering → beans and other legumes put nitrogen back and break pest cycles.
  • Saves water by reusing pond water and skipping irrigation before rain.
  • Recycles nutrients that are otherwise lost, and protects soil from salt.
  • Supports — never replaces — the farmer’s judgment and local experts.

Next steps

From rules to a field‑tested tool

  • Validate thresholds with aquaculture researchers met at LACQUA 2026.
  • Pilot with a pond–field cooperative in the Bajo Lempa, in Spanish, on a phone.
  • Compare full rotation strategies (current vs. pond‑water vs. cover crop) with farmer priorities.
  • Phase 2: a model trained on farmers’ logbooks. We start with explainable rules because AI models above 90% accuracy in pilots often fall to about 75% on commercial farms (Kabir et al., 2026).
